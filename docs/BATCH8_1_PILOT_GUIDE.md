# NexLearn — Facilitator pilot guide (Batch 8.1)

A practical guide for the first controlled classroom pilot. Students lead **NexLearn**, a fictional listed education company, for eight quarters (Q1 2023 – Q4 2024) as a five-role executive team.

## 1. Before the session (allow 30 minutes the day before)

| Step | What to do |
|---|---|
| Environment | Use the deployment and database the product owner designates for the pilot. Do not run a pilot against a preview that writes to the production database unless that has been approved. |
| Database version | Check that the database has the Batch 7 and Batch 7.5 updates. Without them, sessions fall back to MBA mode and **private voting by all five roles** (no single team commitment). Facilitator setup shows a warning when this happens. |
| Rehearse | Create a test session with one team and play Q1 on a laptop (10 minutes). |
| Devices | **One shared device per team (recommended)**, ideally a laptop connected to a screen the team can see. Phones work for reading, but allocation is easier on a laptop. |
| Room | Teams of 3–6 seated together. A projector for your facilitator view. |
| Print | Optionally print the student instructions below. |

## 2. Group size and roles

| Team size | How roles are covered |
|---|---|
| 3 | Each student holds one or two roles. Suggested: CEO + Growth, CFO + People, Product. |
| 4 | Suggested: CEO, CFO, Product, Growth + People. |
| 5 | One role each. |
| 6 | One role each, plus a second Growth or Product lead who challenges the plan. |

- **One shared device per team (default, recommended).** The device is passed so each role reads its own confidential briefing. One student can open several briefings in turn.
- **One device per player.** Each student joins with a role. **Someone must join as CEO**, because only the CEO's device can commit. Roles can be duplicated (two Growth devices are fine). A role nobody joins simply goes unread. This mode was tested with 3, 4 and 6 players.

Executive scorecards and awards describe **how the company performed under each function**. They never measure an individual student's contribution. Say this explicitly in class.

## 3. Session timing

Eight full quarters with discussion take about **90–110 minutes**. Choose one format:

| Format | Plan |
|---|---|
| **Two sessions of 60–75 minutes (recommended)** | Session 1: introduction (10 min), Q1–Q4 (about 12 min each). Session 2: Q5–Q8 (about 12 min each), board report and debrief (15–20 min). |
| One 90-minute session | Introduction (5 min), Q1–Q8 at about 9 minutes each, a 10-minute debrief. Keep "Discuss & Decide" to 5 minutes. |
| One 3-hour block | The comfortable pace, including a full board defense. |

Per quarter:

| Stage | Time | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| Understand | 2–3 min | Briefings |
| Discuss & Decide | 4–6 min | Discussion and allocation |
| Commit | 1 min | Team commitment |
| Results & Learn | 2–3 min | Level 1 briefing and reflection |

## 4. Running the session

1. **Create the classroom.** Home → Facilitator: new session.
   - Enter your email and the number of teams; name them.
   - Choose **Undergraduate** or **MBA**.
   - Keep **Team commitment** and **facilitator-led pacing**.
   - Record the session code and admin PIN. The PIN is never shown in projector view.
2. **Share team codes.** Each team joins with Home → Join as a team → session code + team code.
3. **Introduction.** Each team sees the NexLearn introduction once. Point out the board's one fixed date: a committed strategy at the Q4 2023 meeting.
4. **Each quarter**, teams work through the four stages: Understand → Discuss & Decide → Commit → Results & Learn.
   - In facilitator-led pacing, teams that finish wait until you press **Open [next quarter]** on the Live progress tab.
   - The live table shows each team's stage and quarter.
5. **Projector view** hides the PIN and enlarges the type. Use it for Compare teams and Leadership and awards.
6. **Comparisons** (Leadership and awards tab) always compare teams at the latest quarter every team has completed. Awards appear once at least three teams have completed four quarters.
7. **Final report.** After Q8 each team gets the board report: a board verdict, four lenses, nine sections and the board defense. **Print or save as PDF** from the report.
8. **Debrief tab.** Each team's committed decisions, how each quarter was committed, and discussion questions.

### Facilitator checkpoints

| When | Check |
|---|---|
| After Q1 | Every team reached "Results". If a team is stuck on "Every leader reads their brief", they need to open the remaining role briefings. |
| Before Q4 | Remind teams that the board expects a committed strategic direction at the Q4 2023 meeting. |
| After Q4 | Open Leadership and awards to see the first comparison. Do not announce awards yet. |
| After Q6 | Ask each CFO to explain the team's liquidity position (Level 1 shows cash and the board question). |
| After Q8 | Board defense: each team presents for three minutes using the printed prompts. Then announce awards, stressing that they belong to the team's function. |

## 5. Student instructions (read aloud or print)

> You are NexLearn's executive team. Each of you holds a role — CEO, CFO, Product, Growth, People — and sees confidential information the others do not. Read your brief, then argue for what your function needs. There is no single correct allocation. Every quarter you must agree one plan and commit it as a team. After each quarter, read the three developments and the board's question before you reflect. Your company's share price starts at $100; the board will ask what value you created and whether it is sustainable.

## 6. Debrief questions

- Which decision most shaped your result? Was it visible as important when you made it?
- Where did your functions disagree? How did you resolve it, and would you resolve it the same way again?
- Your company's four lenses — terminal score, share price, functional scores and board assessment — tell different stories. Which is the fairest picture of your leadership?
- What did you learn about timing: capability takes one to three quarters to mature?
- Did any team succeed with little AI investment? What did they do instead?
- What will the next leadership team inherit from you?

## 7. Troubleshooting

| Symptom | Likely cause | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| A team says "Waiting for the facilitator to open Q…" | Facilitator-led pacing | Press Open [next quarter] on the Live progress tab |
| "Waiting for the CEO to commit" (one device per player) | No one joined as CEO, or the CEO device is closed | A student rejoins with the same team code and chooses CEO |
| A team's browser closed or refreshed | — | Rejoin with the same session and team codes; the exact step and draft are restored |
| The network dropped during commit | The commit did not reach the server | Reconnect and press Commit again; a quarter can never be committed twice |
| Two devices pressed Commit together | — | Only one commitment is recorded (tested) |
| The facilitator closed their browser | — | Home → Facilitator: resume → session code + admin PIN |
| Undergraduate or Team Commitment options are missing | Database without the Batch 7 / 7.5 updates | The session runs in MBA mode with private voting (all five roles vote) |
| The awards panel says it needs more teams or quarters | Fewer than 3 teams, or a team has completed fewer than 4 quarters | Expected; awards appear once the rule is met |
| A team asks why its share price barely moved in Q1–Q2 | Investment matures over 1–3 quarters | Expected: early quarters build capability |

## 8. Pilot feedback to collect

- **Timing:** minutes per stage per quarter (note the start and end of Discuss & Decide for two teams).
- **Comprehension:** after Q2, ask each team to state in one sentence what the Level 1 briefing told them.
- **Discussion quality:**
  - Did the competing executive considerations change any allocation?
  - Did any role argue from its confidential brief?
- **Scorecards and awards:** did the awarded function match your own observation of the team? Note any award that felt unfair, and why.
- **Board report:** which section did teams use in the board defense?
- **Technical:** every stuck screen, with team name, quarter and stage; any message shown in red.
- **Students:** a three-question survey after the session:
  - clarity;
  - whether there was a "right answer";
  - what they would do differently.
