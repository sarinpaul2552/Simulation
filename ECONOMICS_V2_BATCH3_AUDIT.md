## Ten calibration questions

1. **Consumer AI dominance across unrelated histories — no longer dominant, still modal.** Best destination for 7/11
   histories, by only +0.9…+4.2 points (Batch 2: best for 11/11 by large revenue margins). Enterprise AI wins Enterprise100;
   Balanced edges University100 and Enterprise+AI. Residual edge is structural: Consumer is ~65% of every company's revenue,
   so Consumer-focused access/commercialization apply to the largest base; its Q7 crisis scales with that exposure but is
   remediable.
2. **Enterprise AI — yes, when preparation supports it.** Best for Enterprise100 (+2.1); highest revenue for the Enterprise+AI
   history ($224M vs $217M Consumer AI) and within 0.7 points of the best score there; Enterprise+AI (72.8) and evidence-
   responsive (77.1) are top-tier. Cost of the thesis: contract concentration raises the Q7 client-crisis severity.
3. **University — structurally no within eight quarters.** University100 → University scores 56.1 and is never the best
   destination, even under favourable conditions (university demand 1.25, slow credential competitors for Q5–Q8): the segment
   is ~8% of revenue in a $40M pool, pipeline resolves 20%/qtr and wins go live 2–5 quarters later, so no University outcome
   can emerge by Q8; its own accreditation crisis then removes the small gain. It is resilient (recession −0.4%, Trust 83–96)
   but not strong. Needs a design decision (cross-segment value of credentials, larger institutional market, or a longer horizon).
4. **Premium Human + AI — structurally no (economically).** It builds the strongest organization (Talent ~120, Capacity 100)
   and meets the mildest crisis (severity 0.47), but pricing power ends at 48 (below the start) and price-mix adds only
   $0.5M over eight quarters, while People commitments cost ~$10M/qtr: margin 4–7%. No channel converts Talent/PQ into premium
   price realization and People investment barely builds PQ (+2.2 per $30M). Needs a premium price-realization channel
   (architecture approval), not a coefficient tweak.
5. **Balanced — yes.** 72.6 (exceptional, top tier), never insolvent, mildest volatility; not the winner (Consumer+AI 79.5,
   evidence-responsive 77.1, aggressive 76.8 score higher).
6. **Cash-heavy — yes.** Survives with the most cash ($242M) and no financing, but zero growth (revenue $180M), no buyer
   interest (strategic value < 0.4) and 51.8 overall — opportunity cost is visible.
7. **People-heavy — yes.** Highest Talent and Capacity, Organizational 70, but Financial 29 (margin 4%, debt $52M) → 41.7.
   Converting it into a strategy (aligned post-Q4 spending under a destination) lifts it to 55–60.
8. **Evidence-responsive — yes.** 77.1 beats every stubborn single-bucket strategy (61.8, 51.8, 64.2, 41.7, 56.1, 51.8) and the
   same-destination 50/50 plan (Enterprise+AI 72.8); only the Consumer+AI thesis (79.5) scores higher.
9. **Concentrated vs breadth — yes, when the thesis is right.** Consumer+AI (79.5) > Balanced (72.6); single-bucket Consumer100
   (61.8) and AI100 (64.2) < Balanced.
10. **Same strategy fails without support — yes.** Consumer/AI with low Talent 50.0 (collapse gate) vs Consumer+AI 79.5;
    Enterprise+AI with low CS 61.7 vs 72.8; Balanced with low Execution 62.8 / low Trust 60.2 vs 72.6. Refusing financing turns
    People100 insolvent (30.0, insolvency gate).
